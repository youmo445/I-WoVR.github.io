Intro

A Shared Core Claim

I-WoVR retains WoVR’s core premise: controlling hallucination enables reliable policy optimization in imagination.

ACWMs may generate visually plausible yet physically incorrect rollouts, resulting in spurious success signals under the policy’s actions.

If world models inevitably hallucinate, how can RL remain reliable under imperfect imagined dynamics?

Additional Challenges in a Broader Setting

→A broader
interaction setting

Extending the same hallucination-aware framework to modern VLAs requires synchronized head and wrist observations, proprioceptive feedback, and absolute joint actions. Longer tasks also require localized rewards and explicit control over how training opportunities are allocated across stages.

Contributions

A VLA-aligned multi-view world model. Geometry-grounded action and camera conditioning, anchored sparse history, and command-based proprioceptive updates support reliable closed-loop interaction.
Temporally coherent exploration. Chunk-wise Flow-SDE noise suppresses high-frequency action fluctuations while preserving the marginal exploration scale.
Stage-local imagined training. Stage-entry resets, stage-specific rewards, local horizons, and controllable stage sampling extend reliable local imagination to long-horizon manipulation.

From WoVR to I-WoVR

WoVR: The Shared WM-RL Framework

WoVR treats the world model as a generative simulator and controls hallucination at three complementary levels: simulator-level control, interaction-level reshaping, and policy-model alignment.

Extensions and Scope

I-WoVR preserves this reliability-oriented framework while redesigning its learned environment, exploration, and imagined interaction protocol for multi-view VLA policies and long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation.

  • Extended and redesigned. We retain first-frame anchoring and extend it to an anchored sparse history. The single-view, delta-EEF formulation with vector action conditioning is replaced by a VLA-aligned multi-view interface with absolute joint-and-gripper commands, geometry-grounded action and camera conditioning, and command-based proprioceptive updates.
  • New. Chunk-wise Flow-SDE exploration preserves the marginal exploration scale while enforcing temporal coherence across neighboring absolute-action targets.
  • Broadened in scope. We carry KIR’s reset-based principle from task-critical regions into long-horizon, multi-stage optimization, combining data-supported stage-entry resets, stage-specific rewards, local rollout horizons, and controllable stage sampling.
  • Orthogonal prior component. PACE in WoVR addresses policy-model recalibration under policy-induced distribution shift. In this work, the world model remains fixed during policy optimization to isolate the effects of interface design, exploration, and rollout organization.

Methodology

VLA-Aligned Multi-View World Model

  • Geometry-grounded control. Absolute joint commands are projected into per-view action maps. Camera ray maps and view embeddings condition a parallel DiT control branch for synchronized head and wrist prediction.
  • Closed-loop context. A persistent first-frame anchor and sparse recent history preserve scene context and task progress. Proprioceptive feedback is approximated from shifted joint-and-gripper commands.
  • Robust and efficient rollouts. Geometry-aware context corruption and variable temporal strides prepare the model for recursive prediction, while DMD2 distillation enables one-step generation for higher-throughput imagined interaction.

Temporally Coherent Exploration

  • Coherent perturbations. One noise vector is shared across temporal positions at a selected Flow-SDE step, avoiding independent perturbations of neighboring absolute-action targets.
  • Preserved exploration scale. Each action token retains the same marginal Gaussian noise scale. The method reshapes temporal covariance instead of simply reducing exploration strength.
  • Imagined policy improvement. The action chunks drive closed-loop VLA–world-model interaction, and grouped imagined returns provide advantages for GRPO policy optimization.

Stage-Local Imagination for Long-Horizon Manipulation

  • Direct access to every stage. GRPO rollout groups start from recorded stage-entry states and observation histories. Balanced stage sampling provides training opportunities without replaying preceding skills.
  • Local learning signals. Stage-specific completion rewards and short rollout horizons reduce reward sparsity and avoid long cross-stage generated prefixes. Steps after stage completion are masked.
  • One end-to-end policy. All stages optimize the same VLA policy. Resets organize imagined training only; at deployment, the policy executes the complete task continuously without resets.

Policy-Level Utility of the Learned Environment

World-model success rates track real-robot performance across the evaluated policies and tasks (Pearson r = 0.970; Spearman ρ = 0.943).

Synthetic trajectories also improve behavior cloning. Mixing 480 success-filtered synthetic trajectories with 401 real trajectories improves WM-BC success from 71.00% to 91.00% under fixed resets and from 72.78% to 76.56% under randomized resets. The smaller randomized-reset gain indicates that filtered demonstrations provide useful, but incomplete, supervision.

I-WoVR improves policy performance on both RoboTwin tasks and outperforms budget-matched Direct RL under both view configurations, approaching the unconstrained converged baseline.

After 50 WM-RL updates with no further real-robot data collection, success improves on all six tasks. The average rises from 39.2% to 55.8%.

Long-Horizon Policy Learning

Uniform stage-reset training reaches 75% end-to-end success (15/20), compared with 30% for SFT, 50% for full-start training with trajectory rewards, and 55% with stage-wise rewards. Arrival-matched stage sampling reaches only 20%, highlighting the importance of balanced training opportunities across stages.
